# Look development and composition

The first film looked right only after its look was built from the brand's own design system: four generic looks were
rejected first (flat shapes that didn't look polished, a 3D filament with no hierarchy, glossy futuristic glass, a
generated look that read as AI). What worked was the brand's own components and tokens, staged in space, approved from
a moving test.

## The north star

The design north star is The Browser Company's craft and warmth, channelled inside the brand's own tokens: borrow the
feeling (hand-made, warm, every move with a reason), never their brand (motion-systems.md).

## How a look is developed

1. **Start from the brand's own language**: its website's components and tokens, its app's screens, its guidelines.
   Rebuild components for film at film scale; borrow the look language, don't import website code.
2. **Moodboards, then grounded moodboards.** At least two directions, then the same directions grounded in the brand's
   kit (its real references wired in). Ungrounded moodboards drift to generic grey glass.
3. **A moving look test** of 10 to 15 seconds, with sound, in the kit's review sheets. Stills don't count: motion is
   half of a look.
4. **The owner picks.** Open questions (an opening colour, a ground) get their own side-by-side look tests; the
   coordinator confirms the call.
5. **Roll it out only after approval.** A look shown for the first time across a whole film is a look that gets
   rebuilt.

The kit's looks library is the starting point: `flat` (the default until a brand chooses), `paper-2.5d` (only for
brands whose own language is papery), `editorial` (type-led, for statements, kinetic type and stings), `blueprint`, and
the After Effects bridge's looks by name when a look calls for AE.

## Composition

- **Compose whole designed objects in generous space**: complete cards, rows and charts, with one clear focal object
  and a clear hierarchy per frame. A thin line on a big empty field reads as empty; four camera variants of a line
  close-up all failed for that reason.
- **Stage beats as objects**: one card splitting into two identical cards, the broken one falling away, rather than
  tracking close-ups of a line.
- **Every element has a job.** Cut on-screen words and decorations that don't (an unexplained lone word, a line with no
  cause, a decorative underline).
- **One focal card at a time** in a paper look: the previous card steps back into depth of field, with blur only on the
  ones that leave focus. Cards behind the focal card step back and soften with blur; they never thin out with opacity.
- **Supporting elements never cover the hero's words**; they settle around the hero, touching only its edge.
- **A spare statement shot that reads too quiet** gets a gentle camera move, not more elements.
- **First passes tend to be too flat-on and sparse.** Plan stronger camera pushes, depth and a signature before the
  first review.

## Colour and light

- **The accent colour keeps one meaning** for the whole film (the line that matters is always the accent; competing
  lines are ink, never the accent).
- **Status colours are the brand's tokens**, never traffic-light gradients or colour ramps.
- **Depth comes from a soft cast shadow that follows an element's height**, never a glow; the element keeps its colour
  and weight.
- **No radial light or edge shading on flat brand fields**: a soft light on a flat disc read as a glow.
- A signature motif keeps one colour through the film and changes form only at designed moments.

## Settling open questions

When a look question is genuinely open (which ground opens the film, how dense a chart should be), don't argue it in
words: build both in the kit's review sheet, side by side, at full size and in motion, and let the owner choose.
