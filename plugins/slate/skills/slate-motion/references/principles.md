# Animation principles, applied to film and interface motion

## The twelve principles

Thomas and Johnston's twelve, from *The Illusion of Life*: squash and stretch, anticipation, staging, straight-ahead and
pose-to-pose, follow-through and overlapping action, slow in and slow out, arcs, secondary action, timing,
exaggeration, solid drawing, appeal. How they apply in a Slate film:

| Principle | Applied |
|---|---|
| **Timing** | Timing gives movement meaning. A turn with two in-betweens reads as a nervous twitch; a stroke of about 10 frames reads as deliberate. |
| **Anticipation** | Short, in one direction, and never longer than the action it prepares: a tuck before a turn, an inhale before a greeting, a lift before a flight. |
| **Slow in, slow out** | Every segment leaves and arrives at rest (or hands its exact velocity on). |
| **Arcs** | Flights travel on arcs, entrances can spiral in, turns carry parts on circles. |
| **Follow-through and overlapping action** | From whole-frame trails (a spread trailing its growth by 2 frames), never from a wobble on every beat. |
| **Secondary action** | Supports the main move and never competes with it. |
| **Staging** | One clear focal object per frame; the camera and composition point at it. |
| **Squash and stretch** | Not on paper-like marks: paper doesn't squash, and parts keep the brand's shape. |
| **Exaggeration** | Rarely. Delight means more from something that never exaggerates. |
| **Appeal** | The feeling of craft: hand-made, every move with a reason. |

## UX in Motion's twelve

Issara Willenskomer's twelve principles for interface motion: easing, offset and delay, parenting, transformation,
value change, masking, overlay, cloning, obscuration, parallax, dimensionality, and dolly and zoom. Motion supports
usability through expectation, continuity, narrative and relationship.

The caution that matters: **only easing carries over directly** from character animation to interface motion. The rest
of the twelve animation principles need translating, not copying.

| UX principle | In a Slate film |
|---|---|
| Offset and delay | whole-frame staggers, capped, in reading or data order |
| Parenting | a container parents its contents; they share its timing |
| Transformation | a shared element changes form without losing identity |
| Value change | a count-up counts once, from its real value |
| Masking | reveals through a shape, never particles |
| Obscuration | depth of field steps back what isn't the focus |
| Parallax | layers at different depths drift at different rates, from one clock |
| Dimensionality | tilt-to-flat and paper depth, one 3D object at a time |
| Dolly and zoom | one camera move per act; bring a card forward rather than pushing in |

## Checking a move against the principles

For any move that matters, step through: its start, its anticipation, its fastest frame, any overshoot, its settle and
its last frame, with motion blur at delivery settings. If any of those frames looks wrong alone, the move is wrong.

## Restraint

Knowing when not to move matters as much as moving. Even audio-first products script their states rather than moving
constantly; a film that holds still before its big moment makes the moment land.
