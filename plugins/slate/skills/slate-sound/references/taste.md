# Sound taste

The owner's taste, as it was stated and as the films proved it. It's the starting point for every brand; a brand's own
palette can change the materials and the key, never the principles.

## The bar

The approved proof: "intentional", the sound "stuck out but wasn't too loud, and it truly matched exactly what was going
on in the video". Sound effects locked to the picture are a signature the owner loves: every visible beat (a card
landing, a chip flipping, a check, a count) gets a small, quiet sound, within a frame.

## The rules

1. **Pads, small soft felt notes, hollow wood clicks and clacks.** No chimes, no bells, nothing metallic or glass, no
   buzzers. (The first proof was "very chime-heavy"; the approved one had none.)
2. **Designed, not stock.** Build it the way a sound designer would, not from a library of generic effects. Generated
   sound effects and music models only fill a gap.
3. **Real instruments for tonal roles.** A synthesized tonal layer (pads, a hum, felt notes made from oscillators) was
   "the problem… an entire rework". Tonal roles play on real sampled instruments; synthesis stays for air, subs and
   textures where it shines.
4. **Never glide a sustained tone's pitch.** A held tone that slides reads as an air-raid siren. Move between notes by
   crossfading held notes, the way a pad changes chord.
5. **No room-tone drone.** A bed under everything read as "a microwave".
6. **One family per kind of interaction**, each its own material, consistent across a brand's films, with designed
   variation between repeats. "The same tone that plays a million times in a row" is bad.
7. **The voice first.** Effects "a little bit too loud" is a fault; so are effects buried so far under a score that they
   stop standing out. The balance hierarchy is in mix.md.
8. **Restraint.** Only what the eye follows makes a sound.
9. **Silence is a tool.** A held breath before a landing (effects fall silent, the music stops dead), opening out of
   silence, and ending on the last frame.
10. **Big moments get an impact with an echo**, on the score's tempo; rare (at most one per act).
11. **A character's sounds** are soft felt notes (its motif), a small wood tick and soft pads.
12. **Reassurance sounds soft**: privacy and locks are soft wood clacks, never metallic; a negative state is a low clack,
    never an error buzzer.

## Where taste is judged

By ear: the owner listens to proofs and approves them. Measurements (levels against the voice, brightness, repeats,
glides, drones) protect an approved sound from drift; they never approve one.
