# Signature moments and the visionary bar

**The bar:** make beautiful things that would make someone cry. It came from the owner after a logo moment that was
correct but "just isn't enough", and it's the bar for every film since.

## One signature moment per act

- Every act gets exactly one: the beat people will remember, where the act's weight falls.
- It lives inside the brand's own visual language. No glows, flares, particles, confetti or bounce; the moment comes
  from the brand's own objects doing something unexpected and true.
- Name it in the treatment, mark it in the script (the shot's `notes`), and give it room: time on either side, and the
  sound's one big landing (slate-sound).
- A correct but ordinary animation doesn't meet the bar. Hero moments are reviewed against it in the packet, and the
  reviewer grades autonomous films against it too.

## How to find one

If the visionary skill is available, use it before designing any hero moment. The method, briefly:
1. **Reframe.** What is this act really about? What would make the viewer feel it rather than be told it?
2. **Go wide.** Generate ideas through different lenses: first principles, a far field's mechanism (print, film,
   hardware, nature), inverting a constraint, a master's taste, the feeling first, the obvious-in-hindsight future.
3. **Kill the median.** Cut the ideas anyone would list.
4. **Push one to a signature.** Specific enough to build, with the insight nameable in one sentence.

Test what survives: is it visceral at first glance, non-obvious, and real enough to build tonight?

## What worked on the first films (as patterns, not recipes)

- **An object that becomes the answer.** The viewer's question ("what does it do for me?") answered by one object they
  can hold, with a page for every door it opens.
- **A held breath before a landing.** Everything still, an empty slot, then the key mark drops in and the impact echoes
  through the ground (slate-motion, signature-moments.md in that skill, for the staging).
- **A payoff set up early.** An empty slot shown in the first act, filled in the last.
- **Leaving the decision to the viewer.** The last box stays empty, and nobody on screen touches it.
- **Restraint as the moment.** For a weighty line, everything holds still but a slow focus pull that leaves one element
  standing alone.

## Checking the bar

- Would the owner say "I've never seen that", and want it built tonight?
- Does it say the act's one idea without a word on screen?
- Is it the brand's, or could it be anyone's?
- Does the sound land with it?
