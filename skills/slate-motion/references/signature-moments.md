# Staging signature moments

The earlier product-film recipe gave each act one signature moment (slate-story chooses the structure). A useful
landing pattern is stillness before it, one clear contact, an echo after and sound on the same frame. The current brief
can choose another rhythm, sustained energy or several peaks. Glows, flares, particles and bounce are available
creative materials when the selected direction calls for them; their intended use still needs legible, measured motion.

## Anatomy of a landing

A hero moment needs more than a correct animation:
1. **A held breath**: everything still, often an empty slot waiting, the music stopped dead.
2. **The drop**: the key mark lands, on the word, on the brand's own ease.
3. **An impact that echoes**: a ripple through the ground, a ring, hairline echoes, the construction grid flaring once.
4. **A text entrance worthy of it**, landing after the impact, not on top of it.
5. **The camera exhales**: a small release after the hit.

The ending can replay the same component as a bookend (logo-build.md).

## A landing in a paper look

For a papery brand's biggest moment: a held breath with everything still; a press down; on the landing, focus snaps
sharp and the shadow merges; an embossed ripple (a lit crest and a soft trough, no light, no glow) runs through the page
and lifts the line it crosses; a hairline echo ring expands and fades; the page gives by well under 1% and recovers with
no bounce; and the camera exhales. A pressed object can leave a trace where it was pressed (a shallow dish in the page),
so the page remembers the moment.

## Other patterns

- **A signature move can use the signature duration** and follow-through: the leading part first, the rest a beat
  behind, the camera moving with it. A token's explicit `max_per_act` is a gate; an assumed one-per-act quota is not.
- **An impact can be a small, damped camera hit**, with the lines shuddering at the stress point, once per act.
- **Land the impact at the end of a sentence**, so its sound and ripple play in the voice's pause.
- **A world can dissolve into the ground and leave its imprint**: the far distance mists away while the words hold, then
  the ground, leaving a shallow deboss with the signature line on its rim; then the imprint relaxes flat.
- **When a character cites data**, the data travels from its source into the sentence exactly as the voice reaches its
  words, after selection marks wipe around the facts it will cite.
- **To show that something won't be stated**, make it a ghost that never lands: faint and blurred in a dashed slot,
  digits rolling without settling, a thread reaching for a source, finding none and pulling back. The sound gives it no
  landing.
- **For a weighty line**, hold everything still (camera and character) except a slow focus pull that leaves one element
  standing alone.

## Timing it

- Give the moment room: still frames before (the held breath) and a settle after, before anything else moves.
- Everything decorative from the impact settles out before the act's contract frame (at least the last 0.6 s), or it
  blinks out on the cut.
- Each designed sound landing (slate-sound) and its picture contact share the same frame.

## Reviewing it

Review the moment on its own, at full speed and quarter speed, with sound, against the visionary bar: would the owner
say they've never seen that? A correct but ordinary moment is sent back, however clean it is.
