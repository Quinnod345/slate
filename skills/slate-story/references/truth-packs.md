# Truth packs: the rules every line and frame obeys

A truth pack is the brand's truth (from onboarding) plus the film's (`brief/truth.json`). The script gates, the claims
gate and the banned-word gate read it; these rules are what it's for. Builders follow them too: the picture can make
claims just as the voice can.

## Nothing invented

- Every UI element, word and number on screen maps to a citation. Never show UI the product doesn't have.
- A concept screen (something that isn't built) needs the requester's explicit sign-off and a visible "concept"
  treatment.
- Never show a feature the product doesn't have, even if the script asks for it (a download, an integration that reads
  someone else's data). A correction from the requester removes it everywhere it appears.
- Never show as working anything that isn't built, is stubbed, or is off by default.
- Product names follow the product's current code, not older materials.
- When the product won't do something, show the absence (the empty page is the point) and draw nothing that implies
  the missing feature (no dial, gauge or projected number).

## Data

- Data graphics are drawn from the product's real distributions, ranges and clamps, never invented shapes.
- Illustrative graphics that aren't real data carry an "illustration" treatment and never show fabricated percentages.
- Conceptual charts carry no numbers and no axis labels.
- Future or illustrative data points carry no numbers; only real sample readings are labelled.
- A counter for a vague quantity (the voice says "thousands") runs as time and never settles on a precise number.
- Show aggregated data the way the product aggregates it; never single out an individual.
- When sample data must be split in ways the screen never shows, choose the split so every statement on screen stays
  true.

## Sample data

- The film's sample company, people and numbers come from one sample-data record, used everywhere.
- The sample-data label appears on every frame that shows sample data, and only there (not on frames showing product
  facts).
- When real content isn't available (assessment questions, customer records), abstract it (word-shaped bars, blurred
  rows) rather than inventing copy.
- A quote from a sample persona is film copy built from a real source line and the voice's words, shown only inside a
  clearly labelled sample context, never as a real testimonial.

## Quotes and testimonials

- No invented testimonials or customer quotes presented as real; that's also the law.
- A quote in the script names who said it and cites a pinned source, or it doesn't run.

## Partners, press and third parties

- Partner mentions stay inside what's true and approved: a statement in type, with no partner logos or terms the brand
  isn't licensed to use. A seal or stamp reads as an endorsement: set a partnership in type instead.
- Press and research artefacts are drawn as paper with their real titles and dates; for logos you don't hold as licensed
  assets, set the publisher's name as text.
- An analogy to a third-party product carries no bureau name, logo or score range.

## Numbers from older sources

- A figure taken from an older source that can't be verified is flagged to the claim authority before it's used.
- Recheck an original's statistics against a current public source, and cut or correct any that don't match.

## When sources disagree

- The code wins over demos, docs and old films; the owner wins over the code for what's claimed.
- When the voice and the product disagree on a figure, re-voice the line to match the product.
- When the product-truth file doesn't quote the exact copy (a label, a limit, a status), don't write it: show the
  structure without the words and note the gap.
- When the product's code doesn't specify a detail, the brand's own official product mock may fill it, with the source
  noted; the code still wins wherever it speaks.
- A film device that isn't product UI (a fanned hand of cards) carries only names and marks the film has already shown.
- Metaphor devices (a company's week, a playbook) are abstract paper (skeleton bars, numbered rows), never fake product
  UI with invented copy.

## Words the brand avoids

The brand's banned words live in its pack, one list, read by every check: the script gates, the text in the act files,
the captions, the OCR of the rendered picture and the transcript of the mix. Add a film-specific word to the film's
never-claim list rather than editing the brand's list.
